# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Primary:** General contractors, specialty subcontractors, and small construction firms — typically 1–50 employees — who actively pursue government-issued work. They check bid opportunities daily or weekly, often from a laptop at a job-site trailer, an office desk, or on a phone between site visits. They are task-oriented, time-pressured, and document-literate; they know how to read an RFP but do not want to waste time hunting for one.

**Secondary:** Government procurement officers who post RFPs and need contractors to find and respond to opportunities correctly.

**Context:** Mid-afternoon on a work day. Construction email open in one tab, this platform in another. The user wants to know quickly: is this opportunity worth bidding? What's the deadline? Where's the document?

## Product Purpose

FindConstructionBids is a government-RFP marketplace for construction contractors: a place to find, filter, and act on publicly-issued construction and services opportunities. Success means a contractor can go from "I need new work" to "I found a bid worth pursuing and I know the deadline" in under two minutes.

## Brand Personality

**Reliable. Legible. Efficient.**

The tool should feel like a well-organized project-management software built for the trades: no fluff, no marketing gloss, no decoration that slows down the task. The voice is direct and factual. The interface trusts that users are professionals; it does not condescend or over-explain.

## Anti-references

- **Standard government-website aesthetics:** navy blue headers, bureaucratic grey, serif body text, star-spangled graphics. The product is a marketplace, not an agency site.
- **Generic SaaS landing-page design:** hero metrics on dark gradients, glassmorphism cards, animated counters, gradient text. This is a tool, not a pitch deck.
- **LinkedIn / Indeed job-board UI:** employer-brand photography, pastel mood boards, recruiter-speak copy. The audience is contractors, not job-seekers.
- **Heavy construction marketing sites:** orange-and-black, diagonal stripe dividers, stock photos of hard-hats, aggressive CTAs. The tool should feel quiet and professional, not a sales brochure for a contractor.

## Design Principles

1. **The task disappears into the work.** Every element exists to help the user find, evaluate, or act on a bid. If removing it would not break a task, remove it.
2. **Hierarchy over decoration.** What the user needs first should be visually loudest. Deadline, dollar amount, jurisdiction — not the brand's color.
3. **Consistent vocabulary, screen to screen.** The same button shape, the same badge style, the same empty-state pattern throughout. Familiarity is an affordance.
4. **Density is a feature, not a problem.** A list of 40 RFPs should be easy to scan, not be artificially enlarged. Tables at standard density with clear row separation beat card grids for this use case.
5. **Professional without being corporate.** The tone is warm stone and terracotta — fieldwork, not bureaucracy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA compliance throughout.
- Keyboard navigation must be complete; contractors may use the platform on keyboards-only devices in the field.
- Color must not be the sole differentiator for status (use icons and text labels alongside color).
- Reduced-motion preference must be respected.
